What is an AI agent, and how is it different from a chatbot?

An AI agent is a digital assistant that receives a task and works on the information and tools it is connected to. A chatbot, usually, only answers what it is asked in the conversation.

In a business, the difference is between a tool that words an answer for you and an assistant that sorts a list, prepares a draft and waits for your approval.

What does a chatbot do?

A chatbot manages a conversation. You ask it, and it answers from what it knows or from what was loaded into it in advance. Usually it doesn't step out of the conversation to prepare something or to go through the business's information.

What does an AI agent do?

An agent receives a task, works on the information the business approved for it, and returns a result: a list, a summary, a draft or a reply to a patient. It works like an extra team member in the office: it knows the business and the way you work, and you talk to it in the tools you already have.

What is the difference in practice?

AI agentChatbot
What it receivesA taskA question in a conversation
Where the information comes fromBusiness information it is connected to, according to what you approvedWhat is said in the conversation or was loaded in advance
What it returnsA list, a summary, a draft or a reply, and sometimes a message to the teamA reply in the conversation

Does an AI agent replace an employee?

No. An agent takes the repetitive work, and the team keeps what needs a person: a decision, a conversation with a patient, an approval. My agents don't diagnose, don't recommend medication and don't promise a final price, and anything they aren't sure about goes to the team.

What is worth knowing before you start?

  • What the agent does, and what it doesn't do.
  • What information it works from.
  • Where it runs, and who sees the information. The agent is built to run in your business's own accounts, and the information stays with you.
  • What needs the team's approval before it goes out.
